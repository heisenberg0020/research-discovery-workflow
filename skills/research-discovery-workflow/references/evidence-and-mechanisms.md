# Evidence, close neighbors and concrete construction

## Evidence handling throughout the workflow

For a consequential source, state **what judgment it can change**, the exact version and original location, material availability, actual reading depth, key conditions, and its effect on the proposal. Keep this in ordinary notes; no mandatory scoring database.

Separate acquired material from understood material. “Downloaded,” “worker read,” “abstract read,” and “rechecked the decisive method/proof/results myself” are different. Search snippets, abstract claims, private notes and tool errors must not masquerade as full-text evidence. Locate sources through title/author/DOI, official proceedings/publisher, lawful preprints, author institutions, or official code as useful. Try a materially different legal path for a consequential retrieval failure; repeated keywords alone are not a new access route.

For decisive judgments, understand the relevant setup and method, then follow the dependency into assumptions, proof steps, baselines, experimental settings, uncertainty, cost accounting or implementation. Read support and challenge with equal care. Cite original links and useful section/theorem/table locations. Recheck decisive original passages yourself; workers may navigate and preserve details without everyone rereading everything.

Source coverage follows question families, strong alternatives and changing judgments, not fixed paper counts. Use complementary searches, synonyms/alternative formulations, backward references, forward/parallel work and contrary findings. An empty search is not novelty certification. Close an inquiry when additional reading no longer offers a reachable decision-relevant distinction, not merely when a script ran.

## What a close neighbor can and cannot establish

For each neighbor that affects a claim:

1. **Identify the affected claim.** Prior existence, mechanism difference, performance, explanation, boundary, or consequence? A conceptual neighbor, technical competitor and explanatory competitor are not interchangeable.
2. **Establish closeness.** Compare task, information, objective, operations, output, assumptions and setting. Same title/problem does not imply equivalence; different names do not imply different operations.
3. **Establish competitive strength separately.** Which dimension, under what comparable conditions, with what evidence? Account for baseline quality, data/splits, tuning/resources and uncertainty where relevant. Author reports, independent evidence and this run's static understanding are different. Closed source is not automatically weak; self-declared SOTA is not sufficient. Do not rank numbers across incompatible settings.
4. **Establish sufficient solution separately.** What goal and acceptable accuracy/cost/guarantee make a treatment sufficient? Without that standard, say what has been achieved, not “the problem is solved.” Relative SOTA is not an upper bound. Inapplicable assumptions and untested conditions neither prove failure nor establish a valuable gap.
5. **Limit the implication; use the positive content.** A precedent withdraws corresponding firstness, not the whole question. Equivalent operations withdraw method novelty, not every possible improvement. A refuted necessary premise changes the affected hypothesis. Strong prior work becomes a serious baseline, explanation or reusable design principle. Missing comparable evidence leaves room uncertain, not zero or unlimited.

Avoid “there is related work, so do something else” and “they did not test our setting, so we have an innovation.” A worthwhile established problem can support a better competing method; value/explanation research need not outperform the studied method.

## Q3 — Expand and challenge the same concrete mechanism

**Entry:** An evidence-grounded sketch. A title and one-sentence wish are not mechanism prose.

1. Reconstruct the existing working process and locate the proposed change or disputed relationship. Separate author interpretation, demonstrated conclusion and your inference.
2. Build the proposal to the necessary level for its contribution:

| Contribution | Required scientific content |
|---|---|
| A: source of benefit / cost / value | The difference to attribute; competing explanations; what information, sampling, budget, resources and workflow co-vary; concrete controls, residual confounding and discriminating result patterns |
| B: phenomenon and explanation | Defined phenomenon and conditions; variables/relationships; mechanism chain and competing chain; conditional/interventional predictions; observables, identification assumptions and unidentifiable parts |
| C: better method | Inputs/outputs and when information is available; variables, operations/update rules; objective or decision rule; learning/use stages where relevant; borrowed vs new parts; costs, failure conditions and exact difference from serious alternatives |
| Theory or conceptual framework | Objects and assumptions; precise proposed statement or distinction; relation to existing results; possible argument/proof route; unresolved steps; operational meaning, rather than a promise of a better bound |

3. Use prose plus equations, pseudocode or diagrams only where they clarify the actual process. Method improvement does not require an extra causal theory. An explanation need not become a neural architecture. Standard tools are not claimed as newly invented.
4. Trace a labeled **constructed example** or symbolic case from input to output: where do current and proposed processes differ, and when do they coincide? This establishes meaning, not empirical efficacy. Do not generate synthetic performance numbers as paper-only review.
5. Check information timing, estimand/objective/output compatibility, observability, needed labels/oracles, and the difference between “defined but untested” and “not yet defined.” Core missing operations cannot be deferred as just an experiment uncertainty.
6. Apply close-neighbor reasoning to the same version of the full mechanism. Seek meaningful support and a strong challenge, not persona votes on a title. Determine distinguishable predictions; if alternatives predict the same observation, improve the design or admit non-identification.
7. Revise actual relations/operations/claims in response. Record original idea → specific objection → actual change → remaining risk. A narrower title or another caveat alone is not repair.

**Exit:** Concrete mechanism, claim-scoped neighbor effects, consequence chain, distinguishing comparison and revision history, or a precise account of where ideation remains incomplete.

## Q4 — Compare, revise or reselect

**Entry:** Enough scientific content to affect candidate treatment.

1. Compare explanations/operations for the same research object. What changes, what outcome should change, and what justifies expecting it? Compare realistic strong/simple alternatives, not deliberately weakened controls.
2. If an operation is equivalent, a premise false, a derivation wrong, or the repaired scope loses its meaningful consequence, revise that claim. Do not automatically kill the broad question. Keep a worthwhile problem and attempt a substantive alternative; reselect when an actual obstacle warrants it.
3. Allow return to discovery or targeted reading while keeping exposure status. If an independent run has already entered Q6, subsequent revisions stay informed rather than becoming independent again.
4. Use bounded iteration: every further pass should add an operation, relation, discriminating prediction, argument or decision-relevant evidence. After repeated no-progress passes, check that construction was actually attempted, then close with honest partial status. Do not endlessly add qualifications or wait for all literature to be exhausted.

**Exit:** Revised concrete candidates or reasoned partial/reselection outcome. Personal investment ranking is optional; it does not replace technical comparisons.

## Separate five kinds of remaining work

- **Reading:** decisive material exists and is readable but not understood; do targeted reading.
- **Acquisition:** specified material remains inaccessible after proportionate lawful alternatives; bound the dependent claim.
- **Evidence conflict:** sources differ under conditions; align them or preserve competing accounts.
- **Ideation:** the core relationship/operation is still missing; construct or report incomplete, not “only needs experiments.”
- **Validation:** an articulated hypothesis needs new proof/data/experiment; plan it without claiming the result.

Local incompleteness restricts the affected judgment. It need not prohibit explicitly conditional construction elsewhere. Conversely, available decisive passages left unread cannot support a claim of a fully grounded proposal.
