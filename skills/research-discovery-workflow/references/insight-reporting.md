# Q7: a report that teaches the user something

## Requirement

Before this comprehensive report, complete Q7-A's four-question comparison design and real resource assessment for each retained developed direction. Check the actual scheme, not a checklist of headings. Unidentified resources or undefined comparisons mean more targeted work or a progress report, not a claimed complete Q7. Identified but unavailable resources remain explicit conditional readiness, never invented access.

A local formal insight can be shared before the main empirical plan is ready, but call it a progress/partial report, not completed Q7-B. Do not promote a completed sub-comparison into readiness for a different main object. Use the retained scientific question, not report length, to distinguish partial progress from the comprehensive final deliverable.

After Q7-A and the final proposals are saved, give a **substantial, self-contained report in the conversation**, in the user's language. Do not end with “all stages completed,” an output index, or links only. The purpose is to share understanding and invite better scientific thinking, not to certify paperwork.

Adapt length to the substantive findings and any explicit user limit. For a normal full run, use connected explanations with enough detail to understand each developed proposal. A short executive conclusion may lead; it must be followed by scientific substance. Avoid repeating entire appendices, one giant matrix, or every search action.

## Explain six things

### 1. What we now understand about the field

Explain the important goals, current answers and their actual conditions. Which widespread framing was misleading, which historical obstacle has moved, which apparently solved question still has a meaningful trade-off, and which assumed problem lacked evidence? Explain how strong prior work functions, not just its names. Integrate enterprise evidence only where it transfers to the present setting.

### 2. What changed during this run

Use specific before/after reasoning from T/U/S, mechanism construction and Q5-R. Say what was confirmed, revised, connected or left unresolved, with reasons. A mistake corrected on paper is not new empirical support. A real disagreement is more informative than artificial consensus.

### 3. How the candidates emerged

For each developed proposal, tell the chain: motivating field situation or method principle → existing answer → concrete remaining relation/bottleneck → proposed explanation/operation → strongest challenge → present form. Explain why some tempting ideas were merged, repositioned or set aside. Do not replace this with selection scores, chronology of tool calls or “ranked highest.”

### 4. Why the specific research could have value

Explain what knowledge, capability or decision would change **if the contribution succeeds**, for whom and under what conditions. Distinguish the importance of the broad topic from the consequence of this specific remaining opportunity. Say which part is observed, inferred or hypothetical and what could make the opportunity small or unnecessary. Already-studied questions may still support a valuable method improvement; lack of prior work alone does not establish value.

### 5. What exactly we propose and how to distinguish it

Describe the core mechanism in plain language, with technical detail where needed. Explain its serious alternative and a discriminating result or argument. State when existing methods could suffice and what claim is *not* being made. Source links should be near the claims they support, preferably original papers/official technical reports with useful locations; cite sources actually consulted, not inferred titles.

### 6. What to think about next

Use the completed comparison scheme to explain the most informative next action, named resource eligibility/access, justified resource estimates, current uncertainties and maturity. Explain which observation distinguishes the two accounts and what the result would change: method development, mechanism study, adopting a simple existing answer, or a better-matched validation setting. If two passes were used, describe actual Q6 changes and reasons, not just agreement counts. End with one or two thought-provoking questions grounded in the findings, such as which consequence the user most wants to resolve or which competing account would change the project's meaning. Ask optional preference questions without making all progress wait for a ceremonial choice.

## Final content check

Read the chat draft against the final main proposal. Can the user explain:

- What the field already knows and where the relevant answer stops?
- What this proposal specifically adds, and how it operates?
- Why that difference may matter rather than merely be new?
- What strong alternative could remove its value, and what would distinguish them?
- What was actually done and what remains only proposed?

If not, restore the missing scientific substance. Do not compensate with more limitations, headers or attachments. Report work closure, proposal formation, evidence adequacy and validation separately. Say plainly if the concrete proposal objective was not reached.

## Tone

Be clear and intellectually honest, not timid. A bounded hypothesis may be worth exploring; explaining its appeal does not mean declaring it proven. State an actual insight when the evidence supports it, rather than flattening every conclusion into “more research is needed.” Preserve disagreements and negative evidence. Do not promise novelty, SOTA, publication success or general applicability from a planning workflow.
