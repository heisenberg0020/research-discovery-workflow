## S1 — Existing decision comparator

Ross D. Shachter (1999), *Efficient Value of Information Computation*, UAI 1999, pp. 594–601. [Official author university PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~shachter/pubs/valinfo.pdf). Version: eight-page author copy titled `valinfo.tex typeset [1]`; a version number is not displayed. Reading depth: §2, PDF pp. 1–2; §5, PDF pp. 6–7. Decisive passages: decision parents are pre-decision observations; utility is maximized; §5 sums over possible observations after the policy calculation. These establish an existing timing-aware comparator. They do not establish our deadline factorization, novelty, empirical benefit, or adoption. We do not assess the paper's cluster-tree efficiency claims. The PDF qualifies its dollar-value interpretation; our stipulated linear utility/cost model makes no monetary conversion claim.

## S2 — Challenge to ignoring absence

Mark A. Peot and Ross D. Shachter (1998), *Learning From What You Don't Observe*, UAI 1998, pp. 439–446. [Official author university PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~shachter/pubs/LearnNotSee.pdf). Version: eight-page author copy, no revision number displayed. Reading depth: §§2–3.2, PDF pp. 2–4. Decisive passages: model the response/report event; §3 explicitly states veridicality and likelihood-independence assumptions; §3.2 gives a likelihood contribution for no report. This warrants checking the observation mechanism. It does not establish deadline latency behavior, this example's numeric values, current clinical performance, or a field-wide gap. No clinical outcome claim is imported.
